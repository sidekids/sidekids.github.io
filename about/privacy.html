Privacy at SideKids

A child's data stays where it belongs whenever possible: with the family.

SideKids apps store settings and data directly on the child's device. There is no SideKids server and no SideKids account.

Some features still need a connection to other services. SideTube, for example, asks YouTube for videos and thumbnails. YouTube receives technical information in the process, such as the device's IP address. This page explains when such connections happen and which data is transferred.

Principles

1. Data stays on the device whenever possible

Profiles, settings, approvals and watch history are stored directly on the device. They are not sent to a SideKids server.

2. No advertising and no hidden analysis

SideKids apps contain no advertising code, no usage analytics and no crash reporting that sends data out.

3. No SideKids accounts

The apps require no account – not with SideKids, nor with Google or YouTube. For some features, the family can connect its own services, such as a private Nextcloud or Plex server. Their access details stay protected on the device and can be removed in the settings at any time.

4. Children should become more independent

Parents can accompany and limit use. As children grow older they should decide more for themselves – parental features are visible and meant to become fewer over time.

SideTube (iOS and Android)

This section is the privacy policy for the SideTube apps for iOS and Android. Last updated: 22 October 2026. Responsible: Christian-Maximilian Steier (SideKids); address and contact: christian.steier.xyz/impressum.

The essentials

  • What SideTube stores stays on the device. Nobody at SideKids can see this data.
  • To show and play videos, SideTube asks YouTube. YouTube (Google) receives the device's IP address and learns which videos are requested – but no account and no name of the child.
  • SideTube shows no ads of its own and does not analyse usage. In the YouTube player, YouTube may show ads on some videos.
  • Parents can view and delete all data in the app. When the app is deleted, the data is gone.
  • Beyond that, data only leaves the device when parents trigger it: by recommending a video or with the notification to their own Nextcloud.

What is stored on the device

  • Children's profiles: name, age band and settings such as daily limit and quiet time
  • What parents approved or rejected, with notes and the history of these decisions
  • Watch history: which video, for how long and when – for "Continue watching", the parents' overview and the daily limit
  • Children's wishes and the parents' replies
  • On iPhone, the last six search terms per child
  • Titles and thumbnails of approved channels and videos, so the app does not have to ask every time
  • Small settings, such as which child was chosen last and whether captions are on
  • The parent PIN: the PIN itself is not stored, only a check value calculated from it. It is kept in a protected area of the operating system (iPhone) or encrypted in the app's storage (Android).
  • If parents set up the notification: address and access details of their own Nextcloud, also stored protected

How long data is kept

SideTube deletes watch history, search terms and answered wishes after 30 days by itself. Open wishes stay until parents answer them. Parents' decisions stay until the profile is deleted. Deleting a profile deletes its data. Deleting the app deletes everything; on iPhone, SideTube also removes the old PIN on the next installation.

The data is not included in backups (iCloud, computer, Android backup) and is not transferred to a new device automatically. After switching devices, SideTube therefore starts fresh.

Technical details on storage

Cached channel videos are deleted after 30 days. Titles and thumbnails of approved content that are older than 30 days are refreshed or removed step by step once a day; without a network connection they stay until the next update. iOS also keeps thumbnails in the system cache, which the system clears by itself.

iOS: database and small settings live in the app's "Application Support" folder, excluded from backups. The PIN check value and the Nextcloud access are in the Keychain, for this device only. Android: database in app storage; PIN check value and Nextcloud access in encrypted preferences (Android Keystore). Backup and device transfer are switched off (allowBackup, dataExtractionRules).

The PIN check value is a hash (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 30,000 rounds, random salt), not encryption: the PIN cannot be calculated back from it, but it can be checked by trying. With four digits, protection therefore rests mainly on the protected storage location and the lockout after five wrong entries.

What YouTube learns

To show videos, SideTube asks YouTube: for the title and image of a video, for the newest videos of an approved channel and for the video itself. YouTube belongs to Google. Google receives the device's IP address, learns which videos and channels are requested and gets technical information about the device and the app. SideTube transmits no account, no name and no other identifier of the child.

When a child watches a video, SideTube uses YouTube's privacy-enhanced embed mode. Even so, the video player loads programs and videos from Google, and Google may receive information about the device and playback. Which other data the player processes is up to YouTube. The player's cookies do not stay on the device permanently.

When parents open a link to YouTube after entering the PIN, it opens in the browser or, if installed, in the YouTube app; their settings apply there.

Google processes this data under its own responsibility: Google Privacy Policy. Watching YouTube content in SideTube is subject to the YouTube Terms of Service.

Technical details on YouTube

Requests go to oEmbed for video and playlist links and the public RSS feed for channel IDs and newest uploads (www.youtube.com), thumbnails (i.ytimg.com, on Android also yt3.ggpht.com and yt3.googleusercontent.com) and the embedded player via youtube-nocookie.com; its program code comes from www.youtube.com, the videos from Google's servers. Google also receives technical information about the device and browser engine.

A channel entered as an @-address or /c/-address that is not in the SideTube source register is resolved by downloading its public channel page once, with a minimal consent cookie for this single request; on the iPhone this only happens after parents agreed in a prompt, and channels from the source register are resolved through the public feed instead; the app does not accept cookies YouTube sends back. It reads the channel ID, title, image and, where present, the short description, and keeps nothing else. The parent screen says so when such an address is typed, and the channel link with ID avoids the request.

Player cookies: on iOS they live in a temporary store that is discarded. On Android third-party cookies are rejected, and the player's cookies and web storage are erased at the start and end of every playback.

The published versions contain no key for the YouTube Data API. On the iPhone, parents can optionally enter their own key (guide); it is kept only in this device's keychain, is not backed up and is removed when the app is deleted. Lookups for channels, search and playlists then go to www.googleapis.com with that key, together with the app's identifier (bundle ID); Google can link them to the Google account that owns the key. Without a key, only the requests described above are made. Building a published iOS version stops if a key were built in.

Videos from PeerTube (iPhone only)

On iPhone, SideTube can also play videos from PeerTube, a free, ad-free video platform run by many independent operators. SideTube only uses servers listed in the app that parents have not blocked. As soon as parents add a PeerTube channel or video or load an age suggestion containing such videos, the app asks that server for titles, images and videos. The server's operator receives the IP address, learns which videos are requested and gets technical information about the device; the operator is responsible for this.

Technical details on PeerTube

Currently listed: tube.bawü.social, framatube.org and tube.tchncs.de. The suggestion for ages 9 to 11 includes videos from tube.bawü.social. Requests go to the public PeerTube interface (/api/v1/…); as with YouTube, the player uses a temporary store.

Notification about new wishes (optional)

Off until parents set it up in the PIN-protected settings. Then, for each new wish, SideTube sends a short message to the family's own Nextcloud – at most three times a day per child, for example "@anna SideTube: new wish (2 open)", in the language of the device. The child's name and what the child wishes for are not included. Nothing goes to SideKids.

Technical details on the notification

The message is sent encrypted over HTTPS to a conversation in Nextcloud Talk; the app does not accept unencrypted addresses. Setup uses a code pasted in the settings. Stored are the server address, conversation, user names to mention and the Talk bot's key – on iOS in the Keychain, on Android encrypted.

Recommending videos

Parents can recommend a video: on iPhone via Messages or Signal, on Android via Signal or SMS, and on both via „Copy link“. The message contains the title, a link to YouTube and a link that opens the video in SideTube. SideTube does not see who receives it and stores nothing in the process. In the children's area, recommending requires the parent PIN.

The SideTube link goes through this website. If SideTube is installed, the app opens directly. Otherwise the browser shows a short explanation page; it is hosted by GitHub, and GitHub receives the IP address and learns which video it is about (GitHub privacy statement). A video from such a link only goes to the review list after the parent PIN.

Technical details on recommendations

On iOS, the text goes to the system message window for Messages and to the clipboard for Signal. Android hands it straight to Signal or the SMS app; if that app is missing, it is put on the clipboard. „Copy link“ puts the whole text on the clipboard on both platforms, marked as sensitive on Android.

The link is https://sidekids.github.io/add?v=…. So that the system may assign it to the app, Android downloads a verification file from this website on installation and update, iOS does so via Apple. Android asks for the PIN first when the link is opened. iOS already shows a preview with title and image, fetched from YouTube for this purpose, and the profile names to choose from.

Children

SideTube is made for children under parental supervision, including children under 13. SideKids collects no personal data from children through SideTube and passes none on. The requests to YouTube and PeerTube described above are technically necessary for playback. SideTube shows no ads of its own and contains no usage analytics, no crash reporting and no additional analytics or advertising code from other providers.

Legal bases

SideKids runs no server for the app and receives no personal data through the app. What SideTube stores on the device is in the family's hands. The requests to YouTube or PeerTube happen because parents approve and play content; the operator of each service (Google, a PeerTube server) is responsible for that processing and names its legal basis in its own privacy policy. When the contact above is used, SideKids processes the details to answer the request on the basis of Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (interest in answering enquiries), or Art. 6(1)(b) where a contractual relationship is concerned.

Transfers to the USA

YouTube is operated by Google; requests to YouTube go to Google servers, including in the USA. Google states that it bases transfers on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (certification of Google LLC) and, where required, on standard contractual clauses (Google: data transfer frameworks, page effective 23 August 2025). When this website is opened, and when a recommendation link is opened in the browser, GitHub (USA) also receives technical details; see GitHub's privacy statement. PeerTube servers are located in various countries depending on the operator. SideKids itself transfers no data to third countries.

The family's rights

Because the data is on the family's own device, it can be viewed, changed and deleted in the app at any time. Under the General Data Protection Regulation there are also the rights of access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and objection (Art. 21) and, where consent is the basis, withdrawal of consent (Art. 7(3)). The contact above is available for this; SideKids can however only give information about data that SideKids itself holds, and that does not include data from the app. There is a right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77), for example in the place of residence. For data processed by Google, GitHub or a PeerTube server, the respective operator is responsible.

What SideKids does not do

  • Analyse usage to keep children in the app longer
  • Secretly monitor messages, screens or whereabouts
