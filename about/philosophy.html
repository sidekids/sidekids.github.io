Philosophy & Principles

Digital tools that accompany children.

What is SideKids?

SidePhone explores what a child's first smartphone can look like when it is built around the child instead of around platforms. SideKids is the child-centered part of that ecosystem: the apps a child actually uses, the design language they share, the tools parents use to accompany (not surveil) their child, and the educational thinking that connects all of it.

SideKids is not a parental-control suite and not an app bundle. It is the connection between four layers that are usually kept apart:

  1. Technical architecture — offline-first apps, optional self-hosted services, no component that depends on a commercial platform to function.
  2. Child-centered UX — one shared interaction model that works the same in every app, so learning transfers.
  3. Privacy — privacy by architecture, not only by policy: data stays local or in the family's private network; what never needs to leave a device, never does.
  4. Media education — tools before portals, transparency before control, growing autonomy as the explicit goal.

Why a Different First Phone?

SidePhone explores what a child's first smartphone could look like if permanent connectivity, algorithmic feeds and attention-maximizing services were not treated as default requirements.

A child with a SidePhone can still be reachable, listen to music and audio plays, take and manage photos, navigate, learn, watch selected videos, take part in a protected social space, and act with growing independence.

The difference is not fewer capabilities — it is fewer permanent digital stimuli as the starting point.

The default first phone is an adult phone: it assumes permanent connectivity, platform accounts, and apps whose business model is attention. A tool-first phone builds a different mental model instead: need → tool → action → done. Feeds invert this — the device proposes the need. SideKids apps never do.

Educational Principles

  1. Child-centered — the child is the user, not the managed object.
  2. Calm — no optimization for usage time.
  3. Less Online — connectivity serves functions; it is not the ground state.
  4. Offline First — core functions work without network wherever meaningful.
  5. Understandable — interfaces and rules are explainable to a child.
  6. Privacy Friendly — minimal data collection, local processing preferred.
  7. Open — open source, open standards, low platform dependence.
  8. Consistent — learned interactions transfer across apps.
  9. Gradual Autonomy — independence can be extended step by step.
  10. Reversible — restrictions are explainable and can be taken back.

UX Principles

Large Targets · One Primary Action · Predictable Navigation · Low Information Density · Safe Exploration · Visible State · No Dark Patterns · Calm by Default

All SideKids apps share these principles.
